A purifier does not clean a room instantly. It cleans by turning the room’s air over repeatedly, which is why matching the model to the space matters more than any single number.

Shut the door

An open window undoes the work as fast as the filter does it. Every rated coverage figure in this category assumes a closed space.

Size up, not down

If your room sits between two models, take the larger. An oversized purifier runs slower and quieter; an undersized one runs flat out and still falls short.

How the sizing maths works

A purifier is rated for a floor area because the real variable is the volume of air it can move in an hour. Measure the room in feet, multiply length by breadth, and compare that to the coverage range on the model. Indian ceilings are usually close enough to ten feet that floor area is a fair proxy; if yours are notably higher, treat the room as larger than it measures.

Two rules hold up across almost every room. First, size up rather than down — a unit running at a low setting in a room it comfortably covers is quieter, lasts longer and still turns the air over more times per hour than a smaller unit at full tilt. Second, one unit cleans one closed room. Purifiers do not pull air around corners, and an open door to an unfiltered room is, as far as the filter is concerned, an open window.

If your space is larger than 400 sq.ft. or opens onto another room, the honest answer is more than one unit. Tell us the layout and we will say what will actually work rather than sell you one and hope.
