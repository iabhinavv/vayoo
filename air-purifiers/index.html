Sizing is the whole game

A purifier rated below your room size will run flat out and never turn the air over. One rated well above it is money spent on capacity you cannot use. The coverage figure on each model is the honest number to measure against.

Find my fitCompare specs

How to choose, in three lines

Measure the room in feet and multiply length by breadth. Match that number to the coverage range on the model — and if you land between two, take the larger one. An oversized purifier runs slower and quieter; an undersized one runs flat out and still falls short.

Then shut the door. Every coverage figure in this category, ours included, assumes a closed room. An open window undoes the work as fast as the filter does it.

What H13 means, briefly

HEPA filters are graded. H13 is the first grade classed as medical-grade, and it is defined by a test: it must capture 99.95% of particles at 0.3 microns — the hardest size to trap.

Watch for “HEPA-type”, “HEPA-like” and “HEPA-style” on other listings. Those phrases specifically mean the filter does not meet the standard; they are usually H11, which catches around 95%.
