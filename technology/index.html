What HEPA H13 actually means

There is a great deal of noise in this category. Here is the part that is measurable, the part that is marketing, and the part we deliberately do not claim.

H13 is a test, not an adjective

HEPA filters are graded on a formal scale. H13 is the first grade classed as medical-grade, and it is defined by a measurement: it must capture 99.95% of particles at 0.3 microns.

0.3 microns is not chosen because it is the smallest particle. It is chosen because it is the hardest one to catch. Larger particles are trapped by simple interception. Much smaller ones move erratically enough that they collide with the fibres anyway. The 0.3 micron range slips through both mechanisms most easily — so grading a filter there grades it at its worst case.

That is why the number is worth quoting. A filter that holds 99.95% at its weakest point does better than that everywhere else.

The grades, in order

H11
≥ 95% at 0.3µm
H12
≥ 99.5%
H13
≥ 99.95% — first medical-grade
H14
≥ 99.995%

Below H11, a filter is not HEPA at all, whatever the box says.

The words that mean it is not HEPA

This is the single most useful thing you can learn before buying anything in this category. “HEPA” is a standard. These phrases are built to sound like it and are specifically not it:

“HEPA-type”

Not graded HEPA. Typically H11 at best, which catches around 95% — meaning it passes roughly a hundred times more particles than H13 at the test size.

“HEPA-like” / “HEPA-style”

Marketing constructions with no standard behind them at all. If a listing uses these, the filter did not pass the test.

“99.97% efficient” with no size

An efficiency figure means nothing without the particle size it was measured at. Ask at what micron rating — if the listing will not say, assume the worst.

Vayoo states H13 and states the test size, because a grade you cannot check is a decoration.

Why 0.3 microns is the number that matters

PM2.5 — particulate under 2.5 microns — is the fraction small enough to pass from the lung into the bloodstream. It is what India’s AQI is mostly driven by, and it is what an H13 filter is built for. The table opposite puts the sizes in order; note how far below everything familiar the test size sits.

A filter graded at 0.3 microns is therefore comfortably inside the range that does the damage. That is the entire technical case for H13 over H11, and it is why we did not build a cheaper model on a lower grade.

Coverage is the other half

Filtration grade tells you what a filter catches. Coverage tells you how much air it can catch it in. Both matter; only one changes between our models.

gauge

The R-10 shows the room’s AQI on the unit. We would rather you watched the number than believed a claim.

What we do not claim

A HEPA filter is a particle filter. It does not remove odour molecules, volatile organic compounds or carbon monoxide — those need a gas-phase medium such as activated carbon, and no amount of HEPA grading changes that. It does not sterilise a room. It cannot clean air that is being replaced through an open window. And it will not fix the air outside your front door.

Anyone telling you otherwise is selling you a story rather than a filter.
